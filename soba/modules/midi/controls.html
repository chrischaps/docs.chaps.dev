Controls

Module ID input.controls · Category Source

Controls turns the faders, knobs, wheels and strips of a MIDI controller into cables. Each of its four slots, A to D, hears one control and puts it out as a Control signal, so you can patch it wherever a cable goes: one fader into a filter's Cutoff, another into a VCA, a third through an Attenuverter into an LFO's Rate.

MIDI Learn can also turn a knob from your controller, but only a knob that's already on a module. Controls is for when the controller is the source: one move feeding several places, a fader scaled and flipped before it arrives, or a control mixed with an LFO.

It listens to the devices ticked in MIDI In on the toolbar, or to the one chosen under Device in its right-click menu (see Listening to one device), and reads each move on its own sample, as Note In reads notes.

Outputs

PortSignal TypeDescription
A, B, C, DControl (Orange)Each slot's control: 0 to 1, or −1 to 1 when its range is bipolar

The face

Each slot is a slim fader that moves with the hardware, showing what its cable carries. The fill rises from the bottom, or from the middle for a bipolar slot. The cap warms to orange while the control moves, and cools again once it rests. A slot's letter lights up once a cable leaves it. Under each fader is what the slot hears (CC 74, Bend, Mod, Press) and its channel. Hover a fader to read it in full, with the value it's sending now.

When you add a Controls module, its slots already hear a keyboard's own controls, so they work before you learn anything:

SlotHears
AThe mod wheel (CC 1)
BThe pitch bend, from −1 to 1
CChannel pressure
DCC 74, which many controllers send from their first knob

Learning a control

  1. Right-click a slot's fader and choose Learn MIDI. Its label blinks purple, and the status bar asks you to move something.
  2. Move a fader or a knob, bend the pitch wheel or strip, or press a key down on your controller.

The slot now hears that control, on the channel it came on. Learning a slot again replaces what it heard, and Undo takes a learn back. To give up before moving anything, press Escape, or right-click the blinking slot and choose Cancel MIDI Learn. What the slots hear is saved with the patch.

Learning a slot only teaches the slot. Nothing is mapped, and a note you play while it waits still plays.

A slot's menu

Right-click a fader to choose what its slot hears by hand:

ItemChoicesDescription
SourceCC / Pitch Bend / Mod Wheel / PressureWhat the slot hears. For a CC, set its number here too
ChannelOmni / 1 – 16Only hear this MIDI channel. Omni hears all of them. A learned slot hears the channel it was learned on
RangeAuto / 0 to 1 / −1 to 10 to 1 runs from the bottom of the control's travel to the top. −1 to 1 puts 0 at the middle, for a bend or for a fader that should push both ways. Auto is −1 to 1 for the pitch bend, which rests in the middle, and 0 to 1 for everything else
HardwareAbsolute / Encoder (64 ±) / Encoder (1 / 127)For a CC: a knob or fader with ends, or an endless encoder that sends steps. Learning tells them apart from the first few values the control sends

An endless encoder has no ends, so the slot counts its steps instead: each step moves the slot 1% of the way, up or down, from wherever it is, and it stops at the ends. A hundred steps take it from the bottom to the top.

Parameters

ControlRangeDefaultDescription
SlewOff – 2 s20 msHow long each output takes to follow its control. A MIDI CC has only 128 steps, and a filter's cutoff swept by one jumps from step to step with a faint zipper. 20 ms smooths the steps away without making the control feel late. Turn it up for slow, gliding moves, or down to Off for a control that should land at once

Uses

  • A fader on a filter. Learn slot A from a fader and patch A into an SVF Filter's Cutoff. The filter follows the fader with no zipper noise at the default Slew.
  • The touch strips. Slot B hears the pitch strip and slot A the mod strip as soon as the module is added. Patch them into anything: the bend into a filter for a wah, the mod strip into a vibrato's depth.
  • One fader, several places. Patch one output into as many inputs as you like, and put an Attenuverter in front of any that should move the other way or less.
  • Pressure as expression. Slot C hears how hard you press a held key: patch it into a VCA or a filter to swell a note as you lean in.
