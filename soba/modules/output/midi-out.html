MIDI Out

Module ID output.midi · Category Output

MIDI Out plays other instruments from your patch: a hardware synth, a drum machine, or a soft synth in a DAW. A Gate and a Pitch cable become notes, a poly cable becomes chords, and four control cables become CCs. Turn on Send Clock and the patch's Clock goes out too, so the other gear follows Soba's tempo, starts when it starts and stops when it stops.

Patch a Step Sequencer into it and the sequencer plays your synth. Patch a Chord Sequencer and it plays chords.

MIDI Out makes no sound of its own. It works in the desktop app. In the browser it shows what it would send but sends nothing.

Inputs

PortSignal TypeDescription
PitchControl (Orange), polyV/Oct, 0 = C4 (note 60), rounded to the nearest note. Read as each gate rises
GateGate (Green), polyA note starts as a channel's gate rises and ends as it falls
VelocityControl (Orange), polyHow hard each note is struck, 0 to 1 (1 to 127). 0.8 with no cable
CC 1 … CC 4Control (Orange)0 to 1, sent as 0 to 127 on the lane's CC number whenever the value moves

Every input is optional. The module has no outputs.

Parameters

ControlRangeDefaultDescription
PortA – DAWhich device plays. Each port's device is a setting of this computer, not of the patch (see below)
Ch1 – 161The MIDI channel the notes and CCs go out on
ClockOn / OffOffSends the Clock as MIDI clock: 24 ticks a beat, with Start, Stop and Continue
CC 1 … CC 40 – 1271, 74, 71, 10The controller number each lane sends. The defaults are the mod wheel, cutoff, resonance and pan, which most synths answer to

The display

  • The lamp and device, top left. Green while notes are going out, and it flashes with each one. Amber while the port has no device, or its device is unplugged. Red when the device won't open, usually because another app has it. Click the device's name to choose another.
  • The channel, top right.
  • A keyboard of five octaves, C2 to C7, with every note the module is holding lit, so a chord shows its shape. A note beyond either end lights the arrow at that end.
  • Four bars, one per CC lane, showing the value last sent, with the lane's CC number beneath. A lane with no cable is an empty slot.
  • The clock, bottom right: four beat lamps that take turns while clock is going out.

Ports, not devices

A patch doesn't name a device. It names a port, A to D, and this computer remembers which device each port plays. Choose port A's device once and every MIDI Out on port A plays it, in this patch and the next. Share the patch and it plays whatever synth is on port A on the other computer. So it travels without either of you editing it.

Click the device's name on the face to choose. The list shows every MIDI output this computer has, and it updates as you plug devices in. A port's device stays chosen while it's unplugged and plays again once it's back.

On Windows only one app can open a MIDI port at a time. If the lamp is red, close whatever else has the device (a DAW, or the maker's editor app), and MIDI Out tries again within a couple of seconds. To play a DAW on the same computer, use a virtual MIDI port such as loopMIDI: MIDI Out plays the port, and the DAW listens to it.

How it works

In time with the sound. Notes don't go out when the patch works them out. They go out when the audio beside them reaches your speakers, which is your audio device's delay later. So a hardware synth and Soba's own sounds play together, and each note is on time to within a millisecond or two.

Chords. Each channel of a poly cable is a voice. A Chord Sequencer's four-voice cable plays four-note chords, every note starting and ending with its own channel's gate. A one-channel Gate with a poly Pitch strikes the whole chord together. Two voices on the same note keep it sounding until the last of them lets go.

No stuck notes. Notes end cleanly:

  • when the Clock stops, even mid-chord. A gate still held stays quiet until it rises again;
  • when you delete the module or open another patch, and when the audio stops. Soba lets go of every note the module was holding and sends All Notes Off on its channel;
  • when you change the channel, the port or the port's device, on the one the notes started on.

Clock. With Clock on, MIDI Out sends the Clock's tempo and place, with no cable needed:

  • Start when the Clock starts from the top. After a count-in, it comes on the first downbeat;
  • 24 ticks a beat, placed on the beat to the sample;
  • Stop when the Clock stops;
  • Continue when it plays on from where it stopped, after a Song Position telling the other gear where that is. Most gear picks up from the next sixteenth.

A patch with no Clock sends no clock. See Tempo and Sync.

Tips

  • One synth per channel. Two MIDI Outs on the same port, on channels 1 and 2, play two parts on a multitimbral synth or two tracks in a DAW.
  • Clock from one module. Turn Clock on in one MIDI Out per device. Two on the same port would send it twice, and the other gear would run at double speed.
  • Velocity from anywhere. A Step Sequencer's second row, an LFO or a Sample & Hold on Velocity gives a part its accents.
  • CCs for movement. An LFO with Bipolar off on CC 2 sweeps the synth's own filter. An ADSR on the same gate opens it with each note.
  • Bipolar sources go below 0, which sends 0. Use an Attenuverter to lift them into 0 to 1.
